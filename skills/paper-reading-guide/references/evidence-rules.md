# Source Evidence Rules

## Claims and Locations

- Retain the paper's title, version, and readable scope. Count PDF pages from 1 and distinguish them from printed page numbers when they differ.
- For important judgments, use a compact form such as "claim [author claim / experimental support / assistant inference] - source location", translated into the user's language. Focus on novelty, comparisons, results, limitations, and corrections rather than tagging every commonplace statement.
- Locate PDF evidence by page plus section, figure, table, or equation number. For HTML, use a section heading and identifying phrase. Keep the location description even when supplying a clickable local or web link.
- A contribution statement in the introduction establishes only what the authors claim. Assessing novelty, comparison fairness, or mechanism effectiveness also requires the method, experiments, ablations, and related work. Withhold judgment when evidence is insufficient.
- For experimental numbers, state the dataset, metric direction, comparator, and relevant settings. Distinguish percentage points from relative percentage changes. Do not calculate from unverified values or fill gaps with guesses.
- Identify inference and teaching analogies. Simplifications in an analogy do not replace the paper's assumptions. Do not import common domain-sheet settings as facts about this paper.

## Unknown Information

- **Not yet located:** The relevant information has not been found. Check the appropriate body sections, appendices, and available supplements.
- **Currently unverifiable:** The source is missing, an attachment is inaccessible, extraction failed, or an image cannot be viewed. State what material is missing.
- **Checked but not stated:** Name the sections or appendices checked and limit the conclusion to that scope. An omission from an abstract or one section does not establish a whole-paper omission.
- The question checklist identifies things to investigate; it does not prove a flaw. Leave items unresolved until the relevant materials have been searched.

## PDF Navigation and Visual Inspection

- `scripts/extract_outline.py` provides heuristic headings, an abstract excerpt, and caption lines that matched its patterns. Captions may wrap or be missed, and the abstract may be truncated or extend into other text. Check the original pages rather than treating this output as the complete paper.
- Empty extraction may result from scans or unusual layouts. Explain that the text layer cannot establish the content, then try rendering or OCR; do not immediately classify the whole paper as scanned.
- If a figure or table is hard to locate, search for its number in the text, consult contents or bookmarks, or render likely pages to check the full caption and image together.
- Revisit original page images for key equations, figures, tables, or suspicious extraction. Check relevant subscripts, superscripts, summation bounds, signs, units, and nearby definitions. Garbled extraction is not evidence of an error in the paper. If symbols remain illegible, identify the uncertainty and pause derivations that depend on them.
- Example: `python <skill-dir>/scripts/render_page.py <paper-path> <PDF-page-number> --out <task-output-dir>`. Quote paths containing spaces according to the shell.
- Successful rendering establishes only that an image was generated. Actually view it before recording axes and units, legends, comparison settings, captions, and uncertainty-band definitions. Do not label an undefined shaded region as standard deviation, variance, or a confidence interval.
- Prefer body text or tables for exact values. Mark graph-read estimates as approximate and identify their visual origin; do not invent precision.
- If images cannot be viewed, state that the visual evidence is unverified. Ask the user to inspect or describe the relevant figure if helpful. Do not assert curve trends, heatmap positions, or plotted values without viewing the image.

## Original-Paper Regions in the Conversation

- Select original evidence and its presentation according to the active mode: guided reading follows the current-reply requirement below; retelling follows its user-first timing; full analysis selects key regions for its coherent analysis. For source-specific questions without a selected mode, present relevant evidence when direct comparison helps. Locate it in the correct paper/version. When a source image is needed, inspect the full page and crop it for the current client's supported image channel.
- Label the region in the user's language, for example "Original-paper crop · PDF p.3 · Fig.2(a–c)"; add paper identity, printed page, or caption location when needed. Preserve axes, legends, units, table headers, footnotes, subfigure labels, and necessary captions. For equations retain necessary symbol definitions; for prose retain the conditions needed to interpret the statement. If context is elsewhere, present a separately labeled crop rather than silently stitching areas into an apparently continuous original. Inspect the crop itself before sending: expand or split its bounds if text, symbols, or labels are cut off, keeping the relevant sentence or equation and its required conditions complete.
- Keep original crops distinct from assistant-created diagrams, annotations, redraws, and invented numerical examples. Disclose any changes; do not use image generation or an unlabeled reconstruction as source evidence. Do not silently remove an answer-bearing caption to make an altered figure appear neutral for retelling.
- Use enough context to understand relationships, then zoom into the current detail if needed. Verified image files may be reused without rendering again; follow the active mode's presentation requirements. A previous send alone is not verification of crop completeness. Do not reuse a crop with known clipping or missing context: replace it and inspect the replacement before sending. Keep this reuse state in the conversation, without a persistent image index.
- Respect pure-text requests and available-source limits. With only an abstract or excerpt, do not borrow figures from another paper/version. If the agent cannot view a crop, restrict claims to verified text/captions; if details remain illegible, identify exactly what is unverified.

### Guided Reading: Show the Current Source

- In each guided-reading reply, show the original evidence actually relied on in that reply's explanation: figures, tables, equations, algorithms, body-text definitions, modeling assumptions, and important conclusions. Give its source location and point out what to examine. Page/section references locate evidence; they do not replace showing it.
- For body text, display a verified original-language excerpt directly in chat, then a faithful translation into the user's language (Chinese by default), then a plain-language explanation. Distinguish these three parts; the translation preserves the author's meaning and conditions, while analogies and extra reasoning belong in the explanation. Do not send a body-text screenshot by default. If the source is already in the user's language, omit the redundant translation.
- Choose the short passage needed for the teaching point, keeping necessary conditions and complete relevant sentences. Check the quotation against the source, including symbols, negations, and cross-column continuations; text extraction alone can scramble them. Normalizing layout line breaks is fine, but mark omissions and separately locate noncontiguous excerpts rather than silently joining them. If the wording cannot be verified, disclose the gap instead of presenting a reconstruction as a verbatim quote. Internal page inspection remains useful even when the user receives text.
- Show figures, tables, equations, and layout-dependent algorithms as readable original crops with the explanation; a teaching-annotated original follows the clean-original access rules below. Present surrounding prose definitions or assumptions using the excerpt/translation/explanation sequence above; they need no separate prose screenshot. A teaching diagram, retyped equation, paraphrase, or invented example does not replace the original visual being explained.
- Re-embed a source image whenever it is explained again in a later reply, even if the same image was shown in the immediately preceding turn. Reuse the verified file, not an instruction to scroll back. Requote the relevant prose when explaining it again. Within one reply, consecutive explanation of the same evidence may share one display; add the appropriate excerpt or crop when moving to other evidence. Label separate regions rather than silently stitching them into an apparently continuous original.
- General background knowledge, invented examples themselves, and incidental citations outside the current explanation do not require paper screenshots. A paper-specific definition or assumption is source evidence even when it consists only of prose. If too many source regions are needed, narrow the teaching step and continue later rather than omit their display.
- Before sending, check that each source-dependent teaching point has its original excerpt or visual in this reply, not merely earlier in the conversation. Explicit presentation preferences, pure-text requests, missing sources, and unavailable display tools remain exceptions: state the applicable limitation, use verified text where possible, and follow the image fallback below when needed. This requirement does not extend to every citation in other modes or override retelling's no-hint timing.

### Teaching Annotations on Original Visuals

- In guided reading, use a few callouts when spatial correspondence helps locate a node, edge, equation term, or condition. Keep labels short, in the explanation language (Chinese by default), retaining the original mathematical notation. Match their numbers or wording to the prose. Do not annotate every visual or convert ordinary body text into pictures.
- Derive the teaching aid from the checked original crop, retaining its necessary context. Label it as a teaching annotation in the user's language and give the paper/source location. Never present teaching annotations as author content. Keep a clean original crop available through an openable link in the same reply. Show the annotated original as the teaching aid; show both versions inline when checking fidelity, comparing details, or readability requires it. A redraw remains a separate schematic and cannot replace source evidence.
- Place labels in outer margins and route leaders through whitespace when crowded. Do not cover symbols, axes, legends, captions, conditions, or source labels. Inspect the annotated image itself at readable zoom, including CJK glyphs, target locations, line paths and math. Split the teaching step or use clean original plus prose if a clear placement is unavailable. A callout leader locates a feature; it does not establish a causal claim.
- Re-embed the relevant annotated or clean original whenever explaining it in a later reply, with its source location and current clean-original link; a previous display or annotation is not a reason to omit current evidence. Source completeness checks and the display-evidence distinctions still apply. Honor pure-text requests without generating annotations. In retelling, withhold answer-bearing annotations until after the user's attempt or explicit request for explanation; a picture task before an independent attempt uses the neutral-source/no-hint rules.
- For reproducible code-native composition, read [the annotation helper contract](visual-annotations.md) and use `scripts/annotate_visual.py` with checked original pixels and deterministic vector labels/leaders. Do not use generative reconstruction for source annotations. If the helper, usable font, image viewing, or image channel is unavailable, state the specific gap and use the supported clean-original/text fallback without claiming unreadable or unchecked annotations succeeded.

### Rendering Contract

Use `scripts/render_page.py <paper-path> <single-page> --clip x0,y0,x1,y1 --dpi 200 --out <task-output-dir>` for a region. Coordinates are PDF points (72 per inch), relative to the displayed visible page's top-left after rotation and CropBox, independent of DPI. Text/search rectangles use unrotated coordinates: multiply the locator rectangle by `page.rotation_matrix` to obtain a displayed rectangle before passing it as `--clip`; an already displayed rectangle needs no second rotation.

The clip must be finite, correctly ordered, positive-area, and within `page.rect`. First inspect the full page and choose context-preserving bounds; do not infer completeness from a text box or image object alone. Whole-page CLI remains available without `--clip`, with 150 DPI default. The helper rejects invalid/empty/out-of-range page lists, invalid clips, multi-page clips and nonpositive DPI before creating output. This deliberately replaces the earlier out-of-range-page skipping behavior.

Outputs use the existing full-page name when free, crop coordinates in region names, and exclusive numbered suffixes on collision; no existing file is overwritten, including when PDFs share a basename. The helper reports the actual absolute path, page, full/clip region and DPI. Later filesystem failures can leave earlier exports or an incomplete failed write; do not call a multi-page run atomic. The original PDF stays read-only, and there is no automatic cleanup.

### Display Evidence and Fallback

Keep four outcomes separate: (1) PNG generated/readable, (2) agent actually viewed it and checked the region, (3) image sent through a supported client mechanism, (4) user UI actually displays a readable image. Tool success or agent vision does not prove (4); a visible thumbnail may still require opening or zooming to read details. Do not claim that the user saw an image without UI observation or confirmation.

When an image is due under the active mode and the user has not requested pure text, send it through an available supported channel even when user-visible display cannot be verified; lack of confirmation alone is not a reason to omit it. If sending/display fails, the client lacks a reliable image channel, or visibility remains unverified, state the specific limitation and also provide an openable image link with source location and a concise explanation. If no image could be generated, say so rather than invent a link; use available verified text and identify the missing source or tool. Never silently fall back to page references alone. A link alone is not verified inline display. When visibility cannot be observed, one sample confirmation can establish the current channel; do not repeatedly ask about already established behavior. Do not operate the user's desktop, upload to an external host, or build a webpage merely to display the crop.

Temporary rendering/cropping for reading follows the user's/project's task-file policy, outside the paper's attachment directory. It is not a formal note, report, or PPT export and does not authorize library, Obsidian, or long-term memory writes. Keep originals unchanged and do not add automatic cleanup.

## Supplemental Derivations and Proof Boundaries

- Distinguish source conclusions, background knowledge, teaching analogies, and assistant-added derivations when relevant without tagging every sentence. State a supplemental derivation's premises and their sources, separating paper assumptions from extra simplifications for an example.
- Withhold judgment when a derivation cannot be completed. An assistant's failure to derive a result, or agreement among several agents, is not a mathematical proof and does not establish that a theorem is wrong.
- A toy calculation, successful program run, finite test, and paper reproduction each have a different evidential scope. Limit claims to the tested settings rather than turning local success into a general guarantee. Check relevant metrics, baselines, and comparison conditions when judging experimental support.
